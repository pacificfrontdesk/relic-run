Privacy Policy

Relic Run · last updated 13 September 2026

Who we are

Relic Run is published by Oleg Nochka, an independent developer ("we", "us"). Oleg Nochka is the person responsible for the app and for this policy.

The short version

Relic Run has no accounts and asks for nothing about you. Your progress is saved on your device and, if you are signed in to iCloud, copied into your own iCloud account so it follows you between your devices. We never see it. Separately, the game reports anonymous gameplay statistics so we can tell which levels are too hard, and that is all. Nothing we receive can be traced back to you, and nothing is used for advertising or tracking.

Your progress

The game saves the following:

  • Your level, coins, gems, energy, relics and museum progress
  • Your daily streak and expedition history
  • Your settings, including difficulty, sound, ambience and haptics
  • A short record of your recent puzzles, used to adjust how forgiving boards are

This is stored on your device. It is also written to your own iCloud account so that the same progress appears on your iPhone and iPad, and survives a new phone. Deleting the app removes the copy on that device.

iCloud

The iCloud copy lives in your Apple Account, not on any computer of ours. We cannot read it, we are not told that it exists, and no part of it is ever sent to us. Apple handles the syncing under Apple's Privacy Policy.

It is a small file, about two kilobytes, holding only the progress listed above. It contains no name, no email address and nothing you have typed.

If you are not signed in to iCloud, the game simply saves on the device and nothing is copied anywhere. To stop syncing at any time, sign out of iCloud or turn iCloud off for Relic Run in the Settings app. A copy already in your iCloud account stays there under your control, and is removed the way any other iCloud app data is, through Apple's settings.

When two of your devices disagree, the game keeps whichever save has completed more puzzles, so syncing can never cost you progress you have already made.

What we do receive

The game sends anonymous events describing what happened in the game: a level started, a level completed or failed, an expedition extracted, a relic found, a purchase screen viewed. Each event carries the app version, the iOS version and the device model, so we can tell an iPhone problem from an iPad one.

Events are grouped under an identifier that is a one-way cryptographic hash of Apple's vendor identifier, salted before hashing. It lets us count returning players without learning who they are. It cannot be reversed, it is not your Apple Account, and it changes if you delete and reinstall the app.

We never send your name, email, location, contacts, photos, advertising identifier or anything you type. Under Apple's definitions this is Product Interaction data, it is not linked to your identity, and it is not used to track you across apps or websites.

This is processed for us by TelemetryDeck, a privacy-focused analytics service based in the European Union.

No advertising, and no purchases yet

There is no advertising in the game and no advertising software in it. This version contains no in-app purchases. If either changes, this page will be updated before that version ships.

Children

Relic Run is suitable for children. Because it collects nothing personal, there is nothing personal to collect from a child either. There is no chat, no social feature and no way for one player to contact another.

Your choices

Deleting the app stops everything and removes the save on that device. Signing out of iCloud, or turning iCloud off for Relic Run, stops the syncing. Because we cannot tell which anonymous identifier is yours, we cannot pick your past analytics events out of the aggregate, which is the trade that keeps them anonymous in the first place.

Changes

If this ever changes, this page will be updated before that version ships, and the change will be described here rather than buried.
